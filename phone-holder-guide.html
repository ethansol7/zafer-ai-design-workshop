Your idea. Your phone holder.

One hour to explore a direction, learn what drives a model, and prepare a prototype you can keep developing.

The browser playground works locally: recognized design words and a drawn front-view outline control a phone-holder model. It does not call AI. The wider workshop shows where AI concept tools, CAD and human review fit together.

00–05 / Imagine a useful thing

Who is this for, where will it live, and what should feel different? A holder for video calls, recipes, a bedside table, or a creative desk can look entirely different. Measure your phone with its case. Write one purpose and one thing you would like to try.

05–12 / Turn words into choices

Write freely in Imagine. Then find the design cues: a style, shape, mood, color, proportions, or lean. Try “a calm organic sage pebble, 85 mm wide, tilt 20”. Read the feedback: which words actually changed the model? The rest of the brief is saved for later development.

Browse 50 original forms: animals, botanical objects, architecture, waves, loops, playful objects and geometric structures. Combine a form with matte, ceramic, metal, walnut, stone or frosted-resin appearance, body and accent colors, wings, stars, ears, leaves or ribs. Try “a blue ceramic cat with gold wings”, then “make it wider” or “remove the wings”. Organic, cubic, modern, stylistic and utilitarian styles offer further variation. Calm, playful, bold, warm and precise moods adjust balance and proportions. Use any hex color. The last recognized cue in a category takes priority; phrasing such as “not red” is skipped. This is a small, visible vocabulary, not unrestricted language understanding.

In a full AI workflow, a chat tool helps clarify the brief and Krea or Vizcom helps explore imagery. A compelling image still needs measured geometry and testing.

12–18 / Sketch and compare

Draw a large, non-crossing front-view outline in Explore. Lift your pen to turn the actual outline into the back. A closed loop inside cuts a hole; an open line inside becomes a raised detail. You can join nearby strokes, undo a stroke, or keep your last valid model while finishing a drawing. Marks outside the outline remain sketch references. The base and phone-support lips remain in place. It does not turn an arbitrary drawing into an arbitrary 3D object.

Save up to three variations. Compare them for your purpose. What did a change in mood or shape do? Which part would you keep? There is no correct aesthetic answer.

18–38 / Make an editable CAD version

Start with a simple straight-sided cradle in Fusion. The detailed sculptural silhouette is a later refinement. Save a native design with editable history.

  1. Use millimeters. Create a side-view sketch on a vertical origin plane. Draw one closed outline combining a base, a tilted back and a short front lip.
  2. Use the example coordinates below as a construction guide, or measure your own values. Dimension the base depth, height, material thickness, pocket and lean. Keep the profile closed and avoid crossed lines.
  3. Extrude the profile across an 80 mm total width as a new solid body. Check the result with a measurement; symmetric extrusion settings may describe whole length or one side.
  4. Save, then edit one driving dimension. Verify that the model changes predictably. Add small edge rounds only after the main shape works. A front-view cut or later loft can develop a more expressive silhouette.

Fusion’s Extrude documentation explains creating a solid from a profile and the available directions.

38–48 / Check one real thing

Measure phone thickness with the case. The browser compares the pocket with thickness ÷ cos(lean angle), plus a 1 mm teaching allowance. This only checks nominal clearance. Rounded corners, strength, tipping, side overhang, charging access, buttons, layer direction and actual material behavior still need review. The ghost phone is a 150 mm tall visual reference.

48–55 / Export and hand off

Download the STL, editable playground project JSON and design notes. STL stores shape; the project preserves color, prompt, sketch and variations. Open the JSON through Keep → Open a saved project. Browser colors do not select a filament or material.

For native Fusion work, save the design, then use Save As Mesh to export STL or 3MF and select millimeters. Reopen the export in a slicer and confirm the dimensions. See Autodesk’s mesh export instructions.

The STL contains closed component meshes which can overlap. Review or merge intersections in CAD or a slicer; it is not a finished Boolean-unioned production solid. Inspect every sliced layer, orientation, overhangs and support needs before printing. A closed digital mesh is not proof of print success. Start with a fit sample and low-risk test.

55–60 / Explain one choice

Show your favorite variation to a partner. Explain what you wanted, what changed, and what is untested. Tomorrow, revise it after using it—or apply the same thinking to a different small project.

Optional help: Concept Kickstart for visual directions; CAD Sprint for editable geometry; Project Handoff for drawings, illustrations and assembly guides. These services are separate from the workshop.

The public workflow is brief → explore → model → check → handoff. Internal agent prompts and application automation are not shared. For more advanced work, Fusion drawings, Illustrator, InDesign and video help communicate reviewed designs.
