Your first editable object.

A hands-on workbook for the 60-minute Zaffre AI + 3D design workshop.

Build a small cable rest, change it for your own use, and leave with a file you can develop tomorrow. The standard exercise takes place in desktop Autodesk Fusion; the AI tool helps you reason about the brief.

Your output: a brief, an editable Fusion design, a prototype mesh, a measured screenshot, and a test note. The goal is a useful digital starting point. Printing and physical validation come afterward.

Before you arrive

Use an appropriate software license. Krea, Vizcom, Adobe software, a paid AI plan, and a 3D printer are not required to complete the core lesson. Checkboxes on this page are temporary and reset on reload; print it or save your own notes.

00–05 / Choose the problem

Complete: “For [person], I want to [solve this small problem], within [constraint], so that [observable result].”

Example: “For my desk, I want a small rest that gives one charging cable a visible place, within a 60 mm wide footprint, so I can find it easily.” A rest does not guarantee that a cable cannot slide out.

My brief:

My cable diameter or relevant measured size: __________ mm. My success criterion: ____________________.

05–12 / Give AI a useful job

A language model suggests text; an image model explores appearance; CAD defines geometry. None of those alone is a physical test. Ask AI to improve a decision, then check its answer.

Help me clarify a simple desk accessory.
The user and problem are: [your brief].
I measured: [value + units + how you measured].
My constraints are: [space, tools, time].
Ask up to three useful questions. Separate confirmed facts

12–18 / Sketch one direction

Draw a rectangular block with one open channel across its top. Label the overall width, depth, height, channel width, and channel depth. Mark dimensions as measured or proposed. The instructor may show Krea or Vizcom concept imagery; use it to discuss form, never to infer exact measurements.

18–38 / Build it in Fusion

  1. Create and save. Start a new design in the Design workspace. Set document units to millimeters and use parametric/history modeling. Save as ONE-Cable-Rest-v01.
  2. Sketch the base. Create a sketch on the XY plane. Use a Center Rectangle with its center at the origin. Dimension it 60 mm wide and 24 mm deep. The profile should be closed. Finish the sketch.
  3. Make a solid. Extrude the rectangle 12 mm with operation New Body. Confirm the preview before accepting. You should have one solid body.
  4. Sketch the channel. Start a sketch on the top face. Place a Center Rectangle at the center of that face. Dimension it 6 mm along the 60 mm body width and 26 mm along the 24 mm body depth. The channel profile extends 1 mm past each end so the cut opens both sides.
  5. Cut into the block. Finish the sketch. Extrude the channel profile with operation Cut, 6 mm downward into the body. Flip the direction if needed. The preview should remove the upper center strip, leaving the base intact. Confirm and save.
  6. Inspect. Orbit the view. Confirm the channel opens at both ends, the underside is solid, and there is one body with editable sketch and extrude features. Name the body CableRest.

Checkpoint: a base by minute 25, an open channel by minute 33, a saved editable model by minute 38. Skip styling if you need more time.

Stuck? If no profile selects, check the sketch is closed. If the cut adds material, cancel and choose Cut. If it points away from the block, flip the direction. If CAD access fails, use the browser dimension preview and the sample mesh for discussion; the native modeling task remains unfinished.

38–48 / Change it. Check it.

  1. Edit the original channel-width dimension from 6 to 8 mm as a reference revision, or choose a width based on your own measured cable and desired clearance. Save as v02.
  2. Inspect the timeline for failed features. Use Inspect → Measure to confirm the new width and the 6 mm floor. Check you still have one solid body.
  3. Explain why the dimension changed. Record a remaining uncertainty: cable fit, sliding, base stability, or surface finish.

Stretch task: use Modify → Change Parameters to name or favorite driving dimensions. A parameter is a value referenced by geometry, so its change can update the model. If you change the body depth, update the channel sketch length to body depth + 2 mm so it still crosses the part.

My revision and reason:

My measured result: __________. My next physical test: ____________________.

48–55 / Export and hand off

  1. Save the native Fusion design. Where available, use File → Export to save a Fusion archive (.f3d) locally; reopen it to confirm the history is present.
  2. Right-click the body or component and use Save As Mesh. Choose STL or 3MF and millimeter units. A mesh is a prototype/export format; keep your native source.
  3. Open the export in a viewer or slicer. Measure its overall bounds. The starting exercise should be 60 × 24 × 12 mm; your revision may differ. Do not judge scale by how large it looks on screen.
  4. For a later print, select the actual printer, nozzle and material; place the broad flat base on the bed and review the sliced layers. Printing time and physical testing are outside this hour. A profile is not a verified fit.
  5. Save a screenshot of the measured revision and complete the README template below.

55–60 / Explain the result

Share one design decision and one untested assumption with a partner. If a box is unchecked, write the next action rather than calling the design finished.

Tomorrow / Your first independent variation

Spend 30–45 minutes on one new low-risk desk accessory or a useful variation: a wider cable rest, a pen rest, or a labeled organizer. Write a new brief, measure the relevant object, create or change a sketch, check the result, and export it. Show a before/after view and explain your revision without replaying every demonstration step.

Stay with simple, noncritical objects while you learn. Designs involving loads, heat, mains electricity, or safety functions need expertise and testing beyond this session.
